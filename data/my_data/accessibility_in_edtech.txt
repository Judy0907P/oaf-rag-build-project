ACCESSIBILITY IN EDTECH

Accessibility means that people with disabilities can use a tool or
resource as fully as anyone else. In education, an inaccessible course
page or video can block a student from learning the material at all.

WHY IT MATTERS

Students may be blind or have low vision, be deaf or hard of hearing, have
motor impairments that make a mouse difficult to use, or have cognitive
and learning disabilities such as dyslexia or ADHD. Accessible design
also helps many other learners: captions help students watching in a
noisy place or learning in a second language, and clear structure helps
everyone scan content quickly.

STANDARDS AND LAWS

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), published by the World
Wide Web Consortium (W3C), are the most widely used technical standard.
WCAG is organized around four principles: content should be perceivable,
operable, understandable, and robust.

In the United States, the Americans with Disabilities Act and Section 504
of the Rehabilitation Act require schools and colleges to provide equal
access, and Section 508 sets accessibility requirements for federal
technology. Many other countries have similar laws.

PRACTICAL CHECKLIST

- Add alternative text (alt text) to meaningful images.
- Provide captions for videos and transcripts for audio.
- Use real headings and lists instead of only bold or large text, so
  screen readers can navigate the page.
- Make sure every feature works with a keyboard alone.
- Keep enough color contrast between text and background.
- Do not use color as the only way to convey information.
- Use descriptive link text such as "Download the syllabus" instead of
  "click here."
- Avoid time limits that cannot be extended.

UNIVERSAL DESIGN FOR LEARNING

Universal Design for Learning (UDL) is a framework that encourages
teachers to plan for variety from the start instead of fixing problems
one student at a time. It recommends offering multiple ways to engage
with material, multiple ways to represent information, and multiple ways
for students to show what they have learned.
