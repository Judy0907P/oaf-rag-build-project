# Learning management systems (LMS)

A learning management system is the central web platform a school or
company uses to run its courses. Students log in to find readings, submit
assignments, take quizzes, and check grades. Instructors use it to post
materials, grade work, and send announcements.

## Common features

- **Course pages** that organize content into modules or weeks.
- **Assignments and submissions**, often with due dates, late penalties,
  and plagiarism checks.
- **Quizzes and gradebooks** that score objective questions automatically
  and calculate weighted final grades.
- **Discussion forums** for asynchronous conversation.
- **Analytics dashboards** that show activity, such as which students have
  not logged in recently or which quiz questions most students missed.

## Well-known platforms

Canvas, Moodle, Blackboard, and Brightspace (D2L) are widely used in higher
education. Google Classroom is common in K-12 schools. Moodle is open
source, so institutions can host and modify it themselves; the others are
mostly offered as hosted commercial services.

## Interoperability standards

An LMS rarely works alone. Standards let it connect to other tools:

- **LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability)** lets an external tool, such as
  a coding exercise platform, open inside the LMS and send grades back.
- **SCORM** and **xAPI** describe how packaged learning content reports
  progress, such as whether a learner finished a module and what score
  they got.

## Criticism

Critics argue that many LMS platforms are built around administration
rather than learning: they make it easy to post files and collect
submissions but harder to support discussion, projects, or feedback loops.
Analytics features also raise questions about how much student behavior
should be tracked and who gets to see it.
