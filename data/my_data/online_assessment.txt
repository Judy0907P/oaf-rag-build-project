ONLINE ASSESSMENT IN EDTECH

Assessment is how teachers and students find out what has been learned.
EdTech has changed both how assessments are delivered and how quickly
results come back.

FORMATIVE VS. SUMMATIVE ASSESSMENT

Formative assessment happens during learning. Its goal is feedback: a
short quiz at the end of a lesson tells the student and teacher what to
review next. Low-stakes online quizzes, polls during a lecture, and
practice problems with instant feedback are all formative.

Summative assessment happens at the end of a unit or course. Its goal is
to measure what was learned, usually for a grade. Final exams and
standardized tests are summative.

Research on retrieval practice suggests that frequent low-stakes quizzes
do more than measure learning: the act of recalling information
strengthens memory, an effect often called the testing effect.

AUTOMATIC GRADING

Multiple-choice, numeric, and short exact-answer questions can be graded
instantly. Programming assignments can be graded automatically by running
the student's code against test cases. Free-text answers and essays are
harder. Some platforms use machine learning or large language models to
suggest scores, but these tools can be inconsistent and are usually best
used to assist a human grader rather than replace one.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Online exams make cheating easier to attempt and harder to detect.
Common responses include:

- Question banks that give each student a different set of questions.
- Time limits and randomized question order.
- Remote proctoring software that records webcam video or locks the
  browser.
- Open-book exam designs that test reasoning instead of recall.

Remote proctoring is controversial. Students have raised concerns about
privacy, about software that misreads normal behavior as suspicious, and
about the stress of being recorded while testing. Many instructors now
prefer redesigning assessments over increasing surveillance.

GOOD PRACTICE

Clear rubrics, fast feedback, and a mix of question types tend to produce
assessments that are both fairer and more useful for learning. An
assessment is only as good as the decision it supports: if the results
never change what the teacher or student does next, the assessment is
mostly paperwork.
